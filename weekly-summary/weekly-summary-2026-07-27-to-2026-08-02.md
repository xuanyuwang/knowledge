## Problems and Lessons

- Similar UI discrepancies came from different layers: pinned template semantics, frontend filter drift, backend mixed-unit aggregation, and time-basis differences. Exact request/population reconstruction was necessary before choosing ownership or fixes.
- A completed ClickHouse mutation does not prove durable deletion when an already-running asynchronous writer can insert stale rows afterward; correctness requires ordering or a post-read existence/version guard.
- Distributed-table acceptance time is not local-part availability time. Monitors that query local tables soon after distributed inserts need an explicit lag model or a confirmation window.
- For live ClickHouse sources, create-without-`POPULATE` plus controlled chunked `INSERT SELECT` is safer and more reviewable than relying on a monolithic historical population step.
- CI and local validation were temporarily blocked by unrelated generated-contract drift and private-package authentication; neither blocker justified bypassing hooks or weakening validation.
- Metrics that look numerically equal across products may still represent different populations or units; the 79.1% Behavioral Hints result matching Performance does not prove semantic equivalence.
