# Workstream 2 recurring-strength synthesis

## Objective

Create the first cross-project analysis of recurring engineering strengths, with repeated evidence, limitations, confidence levels, and targeted evidence-recovery tasks.

## Primary system

`train-for-staff`

## Evidence reviewed

- career operating brief and existing Staff portfolio
- user-filter consolidation project
- ClickHouse external-table investigation, design, implementation, and rollout record
- scorecard PostgreSQL-to-ClickHouse investigation and production verification
- Schwab Leaderboard API and metric-semantics decisions
- analytics, scorecard-workflows, and scorecard-data-sync domain references
- Training Simulator reporting design
- existing resume, publication, self-review, and interview-story candidate banks

## Method

- Required at least two materially distinct examples for a recurring strength.
- Separated demonstrated behavior from downstream adoption or organizational impact.
- Recorded counterevidence and confidence for each proposed strength.
- Used detailed project artifacts as evidence and career artifacts mainly for discovery.

## Findings

- The strongest repeated pattern combines cross-system diagnosis, semantic/invariant modeling, and production-minded validation.
- Problem reframing is also strongly demonstrated and connects those three behaviors.
- Durable knowledge creation is repeated, but its adoption and measurable effect on other engineers remain underdocumented.
- Influence through evidence is supportable at project scope; broader cross-team leadership, delegation, and organizational adoption remain medium-confidence or missing.
- Much of the strongest evidence clusters around analytics and scorecard correctness, so breadth should be tested with a strong story from another problem family.
