## User intent

The user wants a recruiter introduction organized around their role in the team rather than a list of completed projects. They proposed ownership of coaching and quality management, and asked how to explain what that ownership means.

## Recommendation

Lead with product context and technical responsibility, explain the recurring contribution, then use one or two projects as evidence. Ownership should describe decisions and follow-through, not merely familiarity with a service or number of features delivered. This does not require claiming a Staff title or organization-wide leadership.

Suggested spoken draft:

> I'm a software engineer at Cresta, where I take technical ownership of core parts of our coaching and quality management product. These are tools that help contact-center teams evaluate conversations and coach agents. My work spans building new workflows, evolving the backend, and making sure the product's data and reporting are trustworthy. For example, I've led backend work on group calibration and addressed consistency problems between our transactional and analytics systems. The common thread is taking a complicated product problem, working out how it should behave across the system, and carrying the solution through to production.

Use “I own the coaching and quality management service” only if that accurately describes the user's recognized responsibility for the whole service. Current artifact evidence more clearly supports technical ownership of core parts. No finding establishes sole ownership, roadmap authority, people management, or complete-service rebuilding.
